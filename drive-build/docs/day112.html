Day 112: Operations

Whole class opening (5 min)

Quick mental sums: teacher says two numbers, class says the total.

Rotation

Teacher guides group A (15 min) while B and C practise independently; then guides B (15 min); then guides C (15 min).

Group A, Beginning

Skill (OPS-12): Understands fractions (half, one-third, one-fourth) and compares simple fractions

Activity (OPS-12-A3): Fraction of a group, 12 min. Materials: stones, slates, chalk, blackboard

  1. Put 12 stones on the table. Find 1/2 of 12: divide into 2 groups. Each group has 6. So 1/2 of 12 = 6.
  2. Find 1/3 of 12: divide into 3 groups. Each has 4. So 1/3 of 12 = 4.
  3. Find 1/4 of 12: divide into 4 groups. Each has 3.
  4. Children solve on slate: 1/2 of 20, 1/3 of 15, 1/4 of 24.
  5. Discuss: which gives a bigger answer, 1/2 of 20 or 1/4 of 20? Why?

Check: Which number is bigger? How do you know?

Group B, Middle

Skill (OPS-19): Reads, writes, compares and adds decimals; converts between simple fractions and decimals

Activity (OPS-19-A3): Ordering decimals, 10 min. Materials: blackboard, chalk, slates

  1. Write on board: 0.5, 0.35, 0.8, 0.09, 0.62. Ask: arrange from smallest to largest.
  2. Tip: make all numbers have the same decimal places. 0.50, 0.35, 0.80, 0.09, 0.62. Now compare like whole numbers: 9, 35, 50, 62, 80.
  3. Order: 0.09, 0.35, 0.5, 0.62, 0.8.
  4. Children order 2 more sets on their slates.
  5. Challenge: where does 0.125 fit among 0.1, 0.13, 0.2?

Check: Read this number and write the next one.

Group C, Advanced

Skill (OPS-21): Uses simple ratio or unitary-method thinking in everyday situations

Activity (OPS-21-A2): Simple ratio problems, 12 min. Materials: blackboard, chalk, slates

  1. Write: Mix paint in the ratio 2:3 (2 parts yellow to 3 parts blue). If you use 6 spoons of yellow, how many blue?
  2. 2 parts = 6 spoons, so 1 part = 3 spoons. 3 parts = 9 spoons blue.
  3. Children solve: ratio 3:4, if first quantity is 12, what is the second? (12÷3=4, 4x4=16.)
  4. Real-life: recipe uses 2 cups flour to 1 cup sugar. For 6 cups flour, how much sugar? 3 cups.

Check: If 3 pencils cost 15 rupees, what do 5 cost?
