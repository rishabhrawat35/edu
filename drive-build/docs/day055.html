Day 55: Operations

Whole class opening (5 min)

Quick mental sums: teacher says two numbers, class says the total.

Rotation

Teacher guides C (15 min); then B (15 min); then A (15 min).

Group A, Beginning

Skill (OPS-04): Solves everyday word problems using addition and subtraction of two-digit numbers

Activity (OPS-04-A1): Shopping word problems, 15 min. Materials: blackboard, chalk, slates

  1. Write prices on the board: rice 45 rupees, dal 38 rupees, oil 27 rupees.
  2. Ask: Amma buys rice and dal. How much does she pay? Children add 45+38 on slate.
  3. Ask: she gives 100 rupees. How much change? Children subtract.
  4. Make 3-4 more problems using the same prices.
  5. Children share their working on the board.

Check: Solve this addition and this subtraction.

Group B, Middle

Skill (OPS-11): Solves everyday word problems using all four operations

Activity (OPS-11-A1): Operation choice problems, 15 min. Materials: blackboard, chalk, slates

  1. Write a word problem: A shop has 48 apples. 6 children each buy 3 apples. How many are left?
  2. Ask: what operations do we need? Multiply 6x3=18 then subtract 48-18=30.
  3. Write 3 more problems mixing all four operations. Children decide which operation to use first.
  4. Discuss: how do you know whether to add, subtract, multiply or divide? Key words: total/altogether (add), left/remaining (subtract), each/every (multiply or divide).

Check: If 3 pencils cost 15 rupees, what do 5 cost?

Group C, Advanced

Skill (OPS-18): Adds, subtracts and compares fractions with like and unlike denominators

Activity (OPS-18-A1): Unlike denominator addition, 15 min. Materials: paper, slates, chalk, blackboard

  1. Write 1/3 + 1/4 on board. Find common denominator: 3x4=12. Convert: 1/3 = 4/12, 1/4 = 3/12. Add: 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12.
  2. Show with strips: fold one strip into 3 and another into 4. Refold both into 12 parts to see they match.
  3. Children solve on slates: 1/2 + 1/3, 2/5 + 1/4, 3/4 - 1/3.
  4. One child explains each solution on the board.

Check: Which number is bigger? How do you know?
