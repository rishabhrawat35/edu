Day 94: Measurement

Whole class opening (5 min)

Show me a coin - what is it worth?

Rotation

Teacher begins with A (15 min); rotates to C (15 min); finishes with B (15 min).

Group A, Beginning

Skill (MEA-06): Measures and estimates length, weight and capacity; converts between larger and smaller units

Activity (MEA-06-A1): Estimate then measure, 12 min. Materials: sticks, ruler or measuring tape, slates, chalk

  1. Hold up a stick. Ask: how many centimetres long do you think it is?
  2. Children write their estimate on slates.
  3. Measure with a ruler — write the actual length.
  4. Who was closest?
  5. Repeat with 3 more objects.
  6. Discuss: 100 cm = 1 metre. How many sticks would make 1 metre?

Check: Measure this - how long is it?

Group B, Middle

Skill (MEA-11): Finds the perimeter of simple shapes and the area of shapes by counting squares

Activity (MEA-11-A1): String perimeter, 15 min. Materials: string, ruler, blackboard, chalk, slates

  1. Draw a rectangle on the board: length 4 units, width 3 units.
  2. Ask: how far would an ant walk around the edge?
  3. Count together: 4 + 3 + 4 + 3 = 14 units.
  4. Children draw 3 shapes on slates with sides marked and find the perimeter.
  5. Measure the perimeter of their slate with a string, then measure the string with a ruler.

Check: Count these objects - how many are there?

Group C, Advanced

Skill (MEA-14): Finds the perimeter and area of rectangles and squares using formulas

Activity (MEA-14-A1): Rectangle perimeter formula, 12 min. Materials: blackboard, chalk, slates

  1. Draw a rectangle: length 6 cm, width 4 cm on the board.
  2. Perimeter = 6+4+6+4 = 20 cm. Shorter way: 2 x (6+4) = 20.
  3. Write the formula: P = 2 x (l + w).
  4. Give 4 rectangles with different lengths and widths.
  5. Children use the formula on slates. Check by counting.

Check: Name this shape and count its sides.
