Day 96: Measurement

Whole class opening (5 min)

What day is today? What day was yesterday? What day is tomorrow?

Rotation

Teacher guides C (15 min); then B (15 min); then A (15 min).

Group A, Beginning

Skill (MEA-06): Measures and estimates length, weight and capacity; converts between larger and smaller units

Activity (MEA-06-A3): Litre converter, 12 min. Materials: bottles, cups, water, blackboard, chalk, slates

  1. Write on the board: 1 litre = 1000 ml.
  2. Show a 1-litre bottle and a small 250 ml cup.
  3. Ask: how many cups in a litre? (4).
  4. Write 3 problems: 2 litres = ___ ml; 3000 ml = ___ litres; half litre = ___ ml.
  5. Children solve on slates.

Check: Measure this - how long is it?

Group B, Middle

Skill (MEA-11): Finds the perimeter of simple shapes and the area of shapes by counting squares

Activity (MEA-11-A3): Perimeter walk, 15 min. Materials: chalk, measuring tape, old newspapers

  1. Use chalk to draw a rectangle on the classroom floor (2m x 3m).
  2. Children walk along the edges and count their steps.
  3. Measure each side with a measuring tape.
  4. Add the four sides for perimeter.
  5. Cover the inside with old newspapers — count how many sheets fit for a rough area.

Check: Count these objects - how many are there?

Group C, Advanced

Skill (MEA-14): Finds the perimeter and area of rectangles and squares using formulas

Activity (MEA-14-A3): Design a garden, 15 min. Materials: blackboard, chalk, slates

  1. Say: you have 24 metres of fencing (perimeter = 24 m).
  2. Design a rectangular garden. What could the length and width be?
  3. Children find all whole-number possibilities on slates: 1x11, 2x10, 3x9, 4x8, 5x7, 6x6.
  4. Calculate the area for each.
  5. Which rectangle gives the most area? (6x6 = 36). Discuss: a square gives the most area for a fixed perimeter.

Check: Name this shape and count its sides.
