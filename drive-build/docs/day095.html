Day 95: Measurement

Whole class opening (5 min)

Estimate: how many hand-spans is the teacher's desk?

Rotation

Teacher guides group A (15 min) while B and C practise independently; then guides B (15 min); then guides C (15 min).

Group A, Beginning

Skill (MEA-06): Measures and estimates length, weight and capacity; converts between larger and smaller units

Activity (MEA-06-A2): Kilogram and gram, 12 min. Materials: bag of rice (1 kg), small objects, slates, chalk, blackboard

  1. Hold a 1 kg bag of rice (or a known weight).
  2. Ask: is your slate heavier or lighter than 1 kg?
  3. Tell children: 1 kg = 1000 grams.
  4. Show a small object that weighs about 100 grams.
  5. Ask: how many of these make 1 kg?
  6. Children list 3 things lighter than 1 kg and 3 heavier on slates.

Check: Measure this - how long is it?

Group B, Middle

Skill (MEA-11): Finds the perimeter of simple shapes and the area of shapes by counting squares

Activity (MEA-11-A2): Square counting area, 12 min. Materials: blackboard, chalk, slates

  1. Draw a 4x3 grid of squares on the board.
  2. Ask: how many small squares? Count them — 12. This is the area.
  3. Draw an L-shape on the grid. Count the squares inside.
  4. Children draw 3 shapes on their slates on a grid and count squares for area.
  5. Ask: can two different shapes have the same area?

Check: Count these objects - how many are there?

Group C, Advanced

Skill (MEA-14): Finds the perimeter and area of rectangles and squares using formulas

Activity (MEA-14-A2): Area formula, 12 min. Materials: blackboard, chalk, slates

  1. Draw a rectangle on a grid: 5 long, 3 wide.
  2. Count squares = 15. Shorter way: 5 x 3 = 15.
  3. Write: Area = length x width.
  4. For a square: Area = side x side.
  5. Children solve 4 area problems on slates.
  6. Ask: if the perimeter is 20 cm, what could the area be? (Many answers possible.)

Check: Name this shape and count its sides.
