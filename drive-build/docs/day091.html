Day 91: Operations

Whole class opening (5 min)

Teacher gives a multiplication; children show the answer with fingers.

Rotation

Teacher starts with group C (15 min); then guides A (15 min); then guides B (15 min). Other groups do independent practice.

Group A, Beginning

Skill (OPS-08): Adds and subtracts three-digit numbers including with carrying and borrowing

Activity (OPS-08-A3): Estimation before solving, 12 min. Materials: blackboard, chalk, slates

  1. Write 389+245 on board. Before solving, ask: will the answer be closer to 500, 600, or 700? Children guess.
  2. Round: 389 is about 400, 245 is about 250. 400+250=650. So about 600-650.
  3. Now solve exactly: 634. Compare with estimate.
  4. Do 3 more: estimate first then solve.
  5. Discuss: estimation helps you catch mistakes. If your answer was 234 you would know something is wrong.

Check: Solve this addition and this subtraction.

Group B, Middle

Skill (OPS-15): Understands equivalent fractions and adds and subtracts fractions with the same denominator

Activity (OPS-15-A3): Fraction comparison game, 12 min. Materials: blackboard, chalk, slates

  1. Write pairs of fractions on the board: 2/5 and 3/5; 1/4 and 1/3; 3/8 and 5/8.
  2. For same denominator (2/5 vs 3/5): bigger numerator wins. 3/5 > 2/5.
  3. For same numerator (1/4 vs 1/3): smaller denominator wins. 1/3 > 1/4. Draw to show.
  4. Children compare 5 pairs on their slates using > or <.
  5. Challenge: arrange 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/5 from largest to smallest.

Check: Solve this addition and this subtraction.

Group C, Advanced

Skill (OPS-21): Uses simple ratio or unitary-method thinking in everyday situations

Activity (OPS-21-A2): Simple ratio problems, 12 min. Materials: blackboard, chalk, slates

  1. Write: Mix paint in the ratio 2:3 (2 parts yellow to 3 parts blue). If you use 6 spoons of yellow, how many blue?
  2. 2 parts = 6 spoons, so 1 part = 3 spoons. 3 parts = 9 spoons blue.
  3. Children solve: ratio 3:4, if first quantity is 12, what is the second? (12÷3=4, 4x4=16.)
  4. Real-life: recipe uses 2 cups flour to 1 cup sugar. For 6 cups flour, how much sugar? 3 cups.

Check: If 3 pencils cost 15 rupees, what do 5 cost?
