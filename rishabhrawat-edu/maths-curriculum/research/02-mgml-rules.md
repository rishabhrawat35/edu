## (a) Pace and scope

**Rule 1. Plan for fewer topics per year than the textbook has, and say so in the curriculum.** A teacher with two or three grades has half or a third of the time per grade, so the syllabus must move slower, not faster.
- Source: Das-2, step "Slow it Down".
- Quote: "the least we can do for our teachers is make them teach 1.75x than 3x. We need to make our curriculum move slower in MG contexts to maximise time on task."

**Rule 2. Slowing down is not lowering expectations. Cut the number of skills, not the standard for each skill.** The aim is that children master a smaller set of basic skills.
- Source: Das-2.
- Quote: this is "not watering down" the outcomes; the focus "shifts from completing the syllabus to children mastering some (basic) competencies."

**Rule 3. A child moves to the next step only after showing mastery of the current one, not because the calendar moved.** Das describes this for TaRL, and RiVER's ladder works the same way.
- Source: Das-2 (TaRL paragraph); Seminar-2004.
- Quote (Das): children are assessed after a period and "if they have mastered a level, they 'move' to the next level." Seminar-2004 says lessons are not tied to fixed time slots, so each child advances at their own speed.

**Rule 4. Do not stop at finding out where a child is. Keep working with the children who are behind until they reach the level.** Assessment without follow-up support does not count as teaching.
- Source: Das-2.
- Quote: "It's no good to keep on assessing the children and knowing their learning levels but to support the struggling learners (in particular) and persist until they reach the right levels."

**Rule 5. Treat level-based grouping as a short-term fix for gaps, and keep the end goal of grade-level foundations.** Das cautions that TaRL does not replace building strong foundations before a child goes up a grade.
- Source: Das-2.
- Quote: TaRL is "at best a short-term measure" and "not a substitute to building strong foundational skills."

## (b) Grouping and levels

**Rule 7. Start every term by checking where each child is in maths, and group children by that level, not by grade.** Das describes the state TaRL practice as a first assessment, then groups by ability, then different teaching for each group.
- Source: Das-2.
- Quote: states "started with an initial assessment of learning levels of children and then grouped them into different 'levels' based on their ability and then peg the instruction to the groups differently."

**Rule 8. Make the level check short and simple, run by the teacher, and test the grade below as well.** Das asks for easy assessments that the teacher can give as part of teaching, and for items at n, n-1 and n-2 levels so that a class of Grade 5 children with Grade 2 skills can be placed.
- Source: Das-2 ("scaffolding (n, n-1, n-2 levels)"); Das-1 for the spread in a Grade 5 class (some children at Grade 2 level, many at Grade 3 or 4, very few at Grade 5).
- Quote: assessment "might need scaffolding (n, n-1, n-2 levels)."

**Rule 9. Define the levels as an ordered set of maths skills that a teacher can check in a few minutes per child, and let a child move up or stay in the group on that check.** For TaRL maths, the commonly described levels are: cannot recognise numbers, 1-digit numbers, 2-digit numbers, subtraction, division. [unverified] I did not open the Pratham or J-PAL pages, so the exact tasks, how many children are tested at once and the pass rule are not confirmed. Check https://pratham.org/about/teaching-at-the-right-level/ and https://www.povertyactionlab.org/sites/default/files/TaRL-Webinar-Session-1.pdf before writing the level test.
- Source: Das-2 gives only the general method (assess, group, reassess, move up). Level names are from my background knowledge, not from a source I read.

**Rule 10. Nali Kali uses a ladder of milestones and cards that children move through at their own pace, grouped by where they are.** [unverified] I did not open any Nali Kali page. Candidate sources from the search: https://thebastion.co.in/s3fs-public/Nali%20Kali%20Enabling%20for%20learning%20Can%20more%20be%20done2013Issue%20XXI53-55.pdf and https://core.ac.uk/works/57217802. The Seminar-2004 article describes the same ladder idea for Rishi Valley (see the section below), and Nali Kali is commonly said to be adapted from it.

## (c) What a teacher-facing plan must contain

**Rule 11. Each daily plan must answer five questions for the teacher: what to teach, how to teach it, how much time it takes, what group B does while the teacher is with group A, and what the teacher checks at the end.** The first four are Das's list. The fifth follows from Rules 3 and 7.
- Source: Das-2.
- Quote: materials should "make the decision making process (what to teach, how to teach it effectively, how much time will this take, how to handle group A while giving task to group B) for teachers easy."

**Rule 12. Give the teacher ready materials (a handbook, child workbooks or cards, activities) and keep them in the plan, instead of asking the teacher to build the plan from a textbook.** Das sets aside the "teacher agency" argument because children are not learning and teachers are struggling.
- Source: Das-2.
- Quote: "Children are not learning, teachers are struggling to teach, what agency are we talking about and for what?"

## (d) What to avoid

**Rule 13. Use assessment only to decide what to teach next. Do not build in reporting, dashboards or tracking of every child on a platform.** Das lists the purposes the classroom check must not serve. For the same reason, do not make the teacher track so many levels or fields that the record takes time from teaching.
- Source: Das-2.
- Quote: the purpose "should never be to 'report progress' to the system," nor to "track the learning levels of all children through a technology-based platform"; "The purpose is just one - to teach (and teach well)."
- Related, on over-tracking: Seminar-2004 has the teacher record each child's position on the ladder once a week. That is one number per child per week, which is a useful size to copy. Anything heavier is [unverified] as a good idea.

Also to avoid, from Das-2: letting children sit passively through another grade's lesson (Das-1 describes this as common), and treating support as a one-time training. Das says classroom visits and mentoring matter more than checklists and apps ("length is mandatory, line is optional").

## RiVER ladder structure

What the sources state:

| Part | What is stated | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Replaces | Textbooks are replaced by a graded series of cards in language, mathematics and environmental studies | Seminar-2004 |
| Ladder | The whole sequence is called the "Ladder of Learning" (also "Achievement Ladder"), shown as a wall chart of steps and milestones in order | Seminar-2004 |
| Milestones | Groups of cards form milestones, which take students from Level I to Level V | Seminar-2004 |
| Units in a milestone | Five kinds of unit: introducing a skill, applying it, evaluating it, remedial work and enrichment work | Seminar-2004 |
| Cards and logos | Cards are linked to the ladder by logos so a child can find their own next card | Seminar-2004 |
| Moving up | After an initial period children move across the ladder on their own, at their own speed; lessons have no fixed time slots | Seminar-2004 |
| Gate | A diagnostic test near the end of each unit decides whether the child goes to remedial or enrichment cards | Seminar-2004 |
| Absence | A child who was absent resumes at the step where they stopped, not at the start of the year | Seminar-2004 |
| Teacher | Facilitator who moves around, explains new ideas, corrects exercises and records progress. Records each child's exact ladder position weekly | Seminar-2004 |
| Monitoring | A group that does not move on the ladder is read as a sign the teacher has been absent. Monthly visits by a resource person include a simple progress test | Seminar-2004 |
| Maths specifics | A maths kit and "metric melas" (community fairs for mass, length and time) are mentioned. No detail on the maths milestones or card contents | Seminar-2004 |
| Grouping | Students are grouped by learning need, with continuous formative assessment and immediate action on gaps; each child follows an individual path at their own pace, with peer support | Das-2, comment by the RiVER co-founder |

Not stated in anything I read, so [unverified]:
