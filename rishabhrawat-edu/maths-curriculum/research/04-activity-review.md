# Activity Review Report

**Reviewer persona**: Kamala Devi — 15 years teaching in a multi-grade primary school, Rajasthan  
**Date**: October 2026  
**Activities reviewed**: 172 (all strands)  
**Overall quality**: Good — usable for pilot with fixes below applied  

---

## 1. Summary

The activity bank covers all 48 levels across 5 strands with 3-4 activities per level. The activities are well-suited for rural village classrooms: they use locally available materials (stones, sticks, bottle caps, chalk, slates, paper, string), require no printed worksheets or devices, and are culturally appropriate for Indian primary schools.

**Key strengths:**
- Concrete, hands-on activities for early levels (stones, sticks, body movement)
- Good progression from concrete → pictorial → abstract within each strand
- Activities are self-contained — a teacher can follow them without external training material
- Mix of individual, pair, small-group, and whole-class formats
- Realistic time estimates (mostly 10-15 minutes)
- Check questions built into most activities

**Overall rating**: 7.5/10 — strong foundation, needs the fixes below before classroom pilot.

---

## 2. Critical Issues

### 2.1 Duplicate activity: OPS-L05-A1 ↔ NUM-L05-A1

Both are titled "Skip counting clap" with nearly identical structures (stand in circle, count by 2s/5s/10s, clap on milestones). OPS-L05 should focus on building multiplication tables FROM skip counting, not just repeating the skip counting exercise. **Fix**: Rewritten OPS-L05-A1 to focus on linking skip counts to multiplication facts using a table on the board.

### 2.2 OPS-L20-A1 — Unrealistic calculation

The activity asks children to compute 1/4 of 6.25 = 1.5625. This four-decimal-place answer is too difficult for grade 5 and will confuse children who just learned decimals. **Fix**: Changed quantities to 3.5 kg + 2.5 kg = 6 kg, and 1/4 of 6 = 1.5 kg — a clean answer that still teaches the concept.

### 2.3 Steps that assume prior knowledge without stating it

Several activities use terms like "expanded form" (NUM-L04-A3), "fact family" (OPS-L10-A3), or "unitary method" (OPS-L21-A1) without a brief definition in the steps. A teacher encountering these for the first time may not know what to do. **Fix**: Added one-line definitions where terms first appear.

## 4. Strand-by-Strand Notes

### NUM (28 activities, 9 levels)
- Well-structured progression from counting (L01) through lakhs/crores (L08) to factors (L09)
- L09 (factors, multiples, divisibility) is dense — 4 activities for a complex topic. Consider splitting into two levels in the next revision.
- Activities consistently use stones and sticks for early levels, transitioning to slate-and-chalk for later levels. Good.

### OPS (63 activities, 21 levels)
- Largest strand — 21 levels, 63 activities. Good coverage.
- Duplicate with NUM fixed (OPS-L05-A1).
- Fraction activities (L07, L12, L15, L18) are well-scaffolded with paper folding → visual → numerical.
- Decimal activities (L16, L19) effectively use money and measurement as contexts.
- L20-L21 (mixed operations, unitary method) are challenging. Teachers will need more time on these.

### GEO (21 activities, 7 levels)
- Compact and effective. Good use of body movement (string shapes, angle arms).
- Symmetry activities (L03, L05) are strong — paper folding and letter symmetry are engaging.
- L07 (reflection, rotation, tiling) is abstract. The rangoli activity is an excellent cultural connection.

### MEA (42 activities, 14 levels)
- Strongest strand for real-world connections (money, market, cooking, train timetables).
- Early levels (comparison, days of the week) are age-appropriate and engaging.
- Good use of estimation before measurement throughout.
- L13 (volume) activities could use one more concrete activity with water.

### DAT (18 activities, 6 levels)
- Smallest strand. Activities are practical and data-collection-oriented.
- L01 (patterns) — the clap pattern activity is excellent for young children.
- L05 (averages) and L06 (advanced patterns) are well-scaffolded.
- Consider adding one more activity for L04 (bar graphs) — it's a key skill.

## 6. Recommendations for Pilot

1. **Teacher training**: Even with clear steps, teachers need a 2-day hands-on workshop to practice at least 10 activities (2 per strand) before they teach them. Focus on: how to manage two batches simultaneously, how to use the rotation, and how to ask check questions.

2. **Material kits**: Prepare a kit for each school with: 200 stones (cleaned), 200 sticks, 100 bottle caps, string (10 metres), chalk (box of 50), extra slates, a 30 cm ruler, a 1-metre stick or string marked at 1 metre, paper (50 sheets), and a set of drawn coins (if real coins aren't available).

3. **Activity cards**: Print or hand-write the 3-4 activities for each level on a card. The teacher picks the card for the day's level and follows the steps. This is simpler than opening a CSV on a phone.

4. **Pilot scope**: Start with NUM and OPS only (91 activities across 30 levels). Add GEO, MEA, DAT after the first 4 weeks once teachers are comfortable with the rotation system.

5. **Feedback mechanism**: After each day, the teacher answers 3 questions: (a) Did Batch 1 finish the activity? (yes/partly/no), (b) Did Batch 2 finish? (yes/partly/no), (c) Any problem with materials or steps? This feeds the AI plan adjustment system.

6. **Activities to watch closely in pilot**: OPS-L18 (unlike denominator fractions), OPS-L20 (mixed fraction-decimal operations), and NUM-L09 (factors/divisibility) — these are the hardest topics and the activities may need further simplification based on pilot feedback.
